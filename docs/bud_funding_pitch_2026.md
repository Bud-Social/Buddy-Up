# Bud (BuddyUp) — Seed Funding Pitch

## The opportunity

Bud makes fitness participation social and local. Most people do not need another workout video; they need a training partner, a welcoming community and a credible path to a coach. Bud combines mutual Bud connections, gym/studio communities, coach bookings, group sessions and local payments into one participation platform.

The initial market is Kenya, starting with concentrated Nairobi partner communities. This is a mobile-native market: Kenya’s Communications Authority reported 83.5% smartphone penetration by June 2025, while Safaricom reported KSh 38.29 trillion in M-PESA transaction value in FY2025. These figures support distribution and payment readiness; they do not replace customer validation. [CA Kenya](https://www.ca.go.ke/mobile-internet-and-tech-services-surge-kenya-digital-shift-accelerates) · [Safaricom FY2025](https://www.safaricom.co.ke/images/Downloads/FY25-Press-Release_May-9-2025.pdf)

## Product and wedge

Bud’s core product loop is: **join a community → connect with two Buds → plan/book an activity → attend → encourage and repeat.**

The first commercial customers are gyms, studios and coaches. They obtain a branded community, scheduled events, client/member communication, booking, programme delivery and a route to measurable retention. Members receive free accountability and discovery; revenue follows through partner SaaS, completed-booking/programme commissions and a clear Plus membership.

The underlying system already includes web and Flutter clients, secure accounts, buddy connections, communities, real-time messaging, live infrastructure, coach/gym flows, a marketplace, payments integrations, moderation and AI services. The funded plan narrows this breadth into a safe, supportable launch.

## Why Bud can win

- **Accountability, not passive content:** mutual connections and planned activity are the primary product action.
- **Partner-led density:** a gym or coach community supplies a real-world cluster, reducing the cold-start problem.
- **Kenya-native commerce:** M-PESA/card collection and local partner support are built into the launch model.
- **Trust as product infrastructure:** age controls, verification, mutual messaging, moderation and clear payout/dispute operations are essential differentiators.
- **A compounding service ladder:** participation data and trusted relationships support Coach Pro, Gym Growth, Plus, workplace wellness and curated benefits without needing to sell sensitive data.

Global alternatives prove parts of the market but leave space for the integrated local model: Strava leads fitness tracking and community, ClassPass leads transactional class discovery, Trainerize supports coach operations, while WhatsApp/Instagram remain the local informal coordination layer. Bud is not trying to replace all of them; it owns the accountability-and-partner community workflow.

## The raise

**Seeking KSh 50 million seed funding** (approximately US$385,000 at a planning rate of KSh 130/US$, for discussion only) for an **18-month runway**. This is a milestone-sized raise: enough to validate the Nairobi model, convert partner pilots, operate safely and establish a repeatable cluster before a larger scale round. It is intentionally below a growth-at-all-costs raise.

### Team funded

The plan funds a lean 6–8 FTE core, supplemented by specialist contractors: product/engineering (3–4), partner success/sales (2), community/growth (1), and fractional legal, finance, security and clinical/compliance support. Founder compensation and executive hiring must remain within the team allocation; no material senior-hire expansion occurs before partner conversion evidence.

## Revenue logic

Bud does not count transaction value as revenue. It earns KSh 4,000–12,000 monthly partner SaaS fees, KSh 1,500/month Coach Pro fees, a 10% commission on completed paid bookings/programmes, and eventually KSh 300/month Bud Plus subscriptions. The conservative base plan projects KSh 2.9m net revenue in year one, KSh 15.0m in year two and KSh 46.9m in year three; it does not assume break-even within three years. This is appropriate for a trust- and partner-operations-heavy marketplace, but it makes disciplined milestone funding essential.

## Risk controls

- Direct KSh payment via licensed providers first; no cash-out/transferable virtual currency until specialist Kenyan advice confirms the model.
- Practitioner, health-data and AI features remain carefully bounded; Bud is not a medical provider.
- Paid growth is constrained until activated cohorts show retention and contribution margin.
- Customer/partner settlement money remains separate from company operating cash.
- Expansion occurs only with anchor partners and country-specific legal/payments review.

## Investment proposition

KSh 50m gives Bud the chance to demonstrate that local fitness accountability can be a repeatable, trusted and monetisable partner platform in Nairobi. The next round should be earned by the evidence above—not by downloads, unverified GMV or an oversized feature catalogue.
