Offer-ready cofounder agreements for the four founding seats alongside the CEO.

## Founding team & ownership (consolidated)

The founding team holds 80% of BuddyUp Ltd. at incorporation, with a **20%
unallocated reserve** for future hires and the option pool. All five founder
seats hold an **equal 16%** — the YC-recommended structure:

| Seat | Holder | Equity |
|---|---|---|
| Cofounder & CEO — Engineering | **Peter Mbugua** | **16%** |
| Cofounder — Legal, Compliance & Research | *(open — this offer)* | 16% |
| Cofounder — Marketing, Sales & Partnerships | *(open — this offer)* | 16% |
| Cofounder — Security Engineering & Trust | *(open — this offer)* | 16% |
| Cofounder — Finance, Strategy & Controller | *(open — this offer)* | 16% |
| Unallocated reserve (future hires / option pool) | held for unanimous founder decision | **20%** |
| **Total** | | **100%** |

- Every founder's equity vests on identical terms: 4 years monthly, 1-year cliff
  (25% retroactive), default-good-leaver with an enumerated bad-leaver list and
  fair-market-value buy-back, and 100% double-trigger acceleration.
- **Equal split.** YC's guidance on equity splits is blunt: the value is created
  by the years of work ahead, not by the idea or pre-founding build; unequal
  splits create a second-class cofounder. The CEO's pre-founding work (the entire
  platform: web, Flutter, backend, LiveKit media, payments, AI services) is
  acknowledged through **role and casting-vote authority**, not through extra
  equity. If any founder cannot accept equal equity, raise it now — not mid-flight.
- The unallocated **20%** is the market range for an early-stage option pool and
  is created at incorporation, so future hires and options are funded from the
  reserve instead of diluting founders; any top-up beyond 20% requires the
  unanimous consent of all five founders.
- All founding seats are part-time (minimum 10 hours/week) at incorporation,
  measured by outcomes, with commitment level reviewed after the foundations
  period (clause 4 of each agreement).

## Decision-making & the CEO casting vote

Equal equity, one accountable operator: the CEO runs the company and holds the
casting vote on deadlocked cross-domain decisions. The analysis below draws on
YC's guidance on equity splits and co-founder conflict (Michael Seibel's YC
Library essays; YC partners' repeated advice that one founder must be the final
decision-maker).

### Principles

1. **Domains decide.** Each founder decides within their own mandate (clause 3
   of each agreement); the CEO decides company-level calls. Clear ownership
   prevents most deadlocks before they exist.
2. **Most disagreements don't matter — decide fast.** YC's guidance is blunt:
   founder arguments are usually over low-stakes calls, and the real damage is
   slowness and resentment, not a wrong choice. The framework optimises for
   decision speed, not perfection.
3. **One CEO, one casting vote.** When founders genuinely deadlock, the CEO
   decides. If founders cannot accept that structure, that is a founding-structure
   problem — surface it before signing, not mid-flight.
4. **Disagree and commit.** After the vote, everyone executes fully; relitigating
   a settled decision is the actual conflict.
5. **The casting vote is accountable, not absolute.** Written reasons, recusal on
   self-affecting matters, reserved matters that require more than one person,
   and CEO removal as the ultimate safeguard.

### How a deadlock resolves (the mechanism)

1. Each side writes its position with reasoning and evidence (one page max).
2. A 48-hour cool-off. Most disputes dissolve here.
3. If not, the **CEO casts the deciding vote, with written reasons** within five
   working days.
4. The decision is recorded in writing; everyone **disagrees and commits**.
5. **Recusal:** the CEO does not cast on decisions directly affecting the CEO's
   own equity, role or removal.
6. **Reserved matters** are never a solo CEO call — selling or dissolving the
   company, removing or adding a cofounder, changing the equity split, removing
   the CEO, or changing this rule itself. These follow the Founders'
   Shareholders' Agreement (unanimity or board process as drafted there).
7. Last resort for reserved-matter disputes: independent mediation as provided
   in the shareholders' agreement.

The mechanism is summarised in clause 3 of every founder agreement and becomes
binding in the Founders' Shareholders' Agreement (Kenyan law — the chair's
casting vote slots into the Companies Act 2015 model articles).

### Options considered

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Unanimity on everything | Rejected — slow, breeds resentment; YC: decide fast, most calls are low-stakes |
| Rotating chair / vote rotation | Rejected — no accountability, unpredictable outcomes |
| External arbitration as first resort | Rejected as first resort — slow and expensive; kept only as the backstop for reserved matters |
| **CEO casting vote + domain ownership + removal safeguard** | **Chosen — YC default; fits Kenyan model articles; carries into the shareholders' agreement** |

## How to use these documents

1. Fill every remaining `[●]` placeholder (candidate name, ID/passport, start date).
2. Send the relevant file together with the latest business model doc
   (`../buddyup_regulated_business_model_2026.md`) so the candidate sees the same reality you see.
3. Give candidates at least one week and access to counsel before signing.
4. On signature of all four, hand the advocate the working draft
   **Founders' Shareholders' Agreement**
   ([`founders-shareholders-agreement-draft.md`](founders-shareholders-agreement-draft.md))
   together with the signed heads of terms — the casting-vote mechanism and
   Reserved Matters list are already drafted there for perfection. These documents
   are designed to slot into that agreement without renegotiation.

## Shared terms across all four agreements

- **Company:** BuddyUp Ltd., incorporated in the Republic of Kenya.
- **Equity:** equal 16% per founder seat at grant, alongside a 20% unallocated
  reserve created at incorporation for future hires and the option pool.
- **Commitment:** part-time founding seats — minimum 10 hours per week, measured
  by outcomes (clause 4 of each agreement); reviewed after the foundations period.
- **Vesting:** all founder equity vests monthly over 4 years with a 1-year cliff;
  default-good-leaver with an enumerated bad-leaver list and fair-market-value
  buy-back; unvested shares on leaving revert to the company; 100% double-trigger
  acceleration (no single trigger).
- **Trial:** the first 90 days are a mutual foundations period — either party may
  end the engagement before the cliff with no hard feelings and no vested equity;
  third-party regulatory delays extend deadlines day-for-day without failing the
  deliverable (clause 6 of each agreement).
- **Interests:** a living declaration of interests (not a blanket conflicts bar)
  — outside work is permitted; disclosure is required only where an interest
  could impair impartiality, divert company work, or place a founder opposite
  the company (clause 7 of each agreement).
- **Director protections:** D&O insurance (or a Companies Act 2015-compliant
  indemnity) is in place before any board appointment; shareholders'-agreement
  advocate fees are a company operational expense (clause 8 of each agreement).
- **Decision-making:** domain ownership plus a CEO casting vote with written
  reasons, recusal, and reserved matters — see "Decision-making & the CEO
  casting vote" above.
- **IP & confidentiality:** all work product assigns to the company from day one;
  confidentiality survives exit indefinitely; fiduciary duty applies while a director.
- **Dilution:** normal — future rounds dilute everyone proportionally; pool
  top-ups beyond 20% or pools from founder holdings require unanimous consent.
- **Not legal advice:** these templates must be reviewed by a Kenyan advocate before signing.
